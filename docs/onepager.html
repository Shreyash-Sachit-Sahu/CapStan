Capstan

Bounded revenue recovery for failed recurring payments
Razorpay AI Buildathon · Track 03
10 fixtures · 3,000 cases · one oracle, both arms

A failed mandate debit is diagnosed, matched to a bounded recovery ladder, and executed exactly once. At 49% of a naive retry ladder’s attempt volume it recovers more money on 10 of 10 batches, with zero duplicate charges against the baseline’s 33 and zero debits against fraud-blocked customers against the baseline’s 270.

Capstan wins wherever the cap binds. The claim is cost-per-recovery and safety, not recovery rate.

Payday timing is worth more than our entire lead. Ablate it and Capstan recovers 23.18% against the baseline’s 30.26% — we lose. That is the load-bearing decision and it is checkable: run the ablation. It is also the first thing we would replace, because payday is inferred from prior successful debits and defaults to the 1st of the month on a cold batch.

Four calls we made against our own interest

Every measurement bug we found had been depressing our numbers — which is structural rather than virtuous, since the baseline holds almost no state to corrupt. So the bugs are not the evidence. These are.

DecisionWhat it cost us
Gave the holdout a bank-narration vocabulary our rules had never seen Reported diagnosis accuracy 0.9533, not the 0.9833 the tuning set scores
Declined to model what a completed re-authorisation refreshes 4.36pp of our own expectation, forgone and stated as a limitation
Left the simulator’s independent-attempt bias in place Correcting it would raise our numbers; it favours the arm we beat
Refused to add a cost model after seeing results Pricing attempts, churn or refunds would flip the ranking our way

Every simulator correction was written down with a predicted direction and committed before it was measured (docs/decisions/). Four of four pre-registered magnitudes then came in below prediction; we report the misses rather than the ranges.

How to check it. Same oracle for both arms, package-isolated from the agent by a test that fails the build if the agent so much as names it. Ceiling published; all 177 non-recoveries published with causes. Raw output in docs/report_holdout.json.

Where the AI is. Diagnosis only. A deterministic map over Razorpay’s source:step:reason resolves 75% of cases at 1.00 accuracy; an LLM classifies the 25% where the bank declined without a reason. PolicyEngine holds no classifier reference — the model cannot reach a decision path.

What is simulated. The Razorpay adapter is written against documented test-mode endpoints but has not been exercised against live credentials — the recurring leg needs a tokenised mandate from a checkout flow that cannot be completed headlessly. Every number here runs through the simulated gateway, and we say so.
